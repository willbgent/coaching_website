Philosophy

How I coach.

A working document, not a finished one. The work is never finished.

“Adapt
or die.”

03 — The Principles

[PLACEHOLDER PHILOSOPHY INTRO — write this in your voice.] Basketball is a game of constant adjustment. Schemes get solved. Players grow. The league evolves every eighteen months. The coaches who matter are the ones who refuse to fall in love with their own answers — who treat every season, every series, every possession as a new problem.

Four ideas anchor the work.

No. 01

Develop the player, not the position.

[PLACEHOLDER] The best player-development programs don't slot people into boxes — they expand what each player can do, then let the role find them. Skill work is a conversation, not a curriculum.

No. 02

Offense is a problem-creation engine.

[PLACEHOLDER] Good offense isn't a playbook — it's a set of principles that create defensive problems faster than the other team can solve them. Spacing, pace, decision speed, and shot quality, in that order.

No. 03

Trust is the only real system.

[PLACEHOLDER] No scheme survives if the locker room doesn't believe in it. Trust is built in the boring hours — the 1-on-1 film sessions, the honest conversations, the consistency between what you say and what you do.

No. 04

Adapt — relentlessly.

[PLACEHOLDER] What worked in November won't win in May. The team that finishes the season is rarely the team that started it. The job is to keep evolving — and to bring the players with you.
